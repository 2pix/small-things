# About

This is here so Black Cat has a home. These pieces arrived unfinished — I have plenty more lying around in folders.

What I found through writing is that I had a way of describing exactly how I see things — somatic first. Sometimes so much information comes in that it's overwhelming, and I learned how to pause with it.

Making sense of the writing also meant reliving and untangling years where things were unprocessed. I was, literally, off on a tangent — too sensitive, felt too much. But I realised it wasn't too sensitive or too much. I discuss many of the things that get my own hackles up.

Some people will see it. Some people will float right past.

Someone said to me that one of my characters was wishy washy. That he said he loved her and then didn't do anything with it. And he wasn't clear about ending it either. That the gap between what he felt and what he did was the gap of a man who wouldn't or couldn't commit to his own life.

I sat with this for a while. Because from the outside it looks like that. He loves her. He says so. Then he doesn't make a move to stay.

But I think there's a difference between avoidance and only claiming what you know is true until the whole truth appears. I think it takes a whole lot of courage — to instead stay suspended in unknowing, and not grabbing to reach an answer. It's infinitely harder when you only have a direction. Not outward facing, but an internal pull that points you in a direction.

You can't say what you don't yet know. You can only say what's true so far and stay honest about the rest being unformed.

*I don't know if that's enough,* she said.

She was asking the right question. He was answering the only one he had an answer to.

I know this because I'm standing in the same place. Not about a person. About my life. There is a tile under my feet that sounds hollow. Not wrong — hollow. As in, there is a gap beneath it that hasn't opened yet. And when it does, the floor will go sideways, the sky will tilt ninety degrees left, and where the horizon was, there will be something that was always there.

You can't claim that in advance. You can only say: I know this much. The rest hasn't arrived yet.

I recognise people will respond to "How to Win a Million Dollars," or "10 Steps to..." This writing is spiral, not rooted in direction apart from the somatic register. Someone I work with saw it immediately.

## Why Git, not WordPress

I've had website experience since 1997, when I built a Macromedia Flash website for my boss. The idea of custom — of fit — was embedded from then.

After 2000 it got harder, because I couldn't code. Being able to now — because I have the fluency and know exactly what I'm looking for — is great. I literally get to concentrate on writing, once the scaffolding is built.

I'm not a coder. I understand the logic behind the code, but ask me to write it from scratch and I get the same issue I get with music.

I wanted a home for this where I didn't have to pay for things. I thought Substack was it — until I found out Substack is walled. People who aren't on Substack can't actually read it.

WordPress or any other builder wasn't the answer either — I couldn't control how each page looked, especially the landing pages. I'd have gotten bogged down knowing I had to keep editing the site, wrestling with CSS manually, breaking WordPress in the process. It would have taken too long.

I also have a thing about having my own slice of the internet that's mine.

I built this with Claude in fifteen hours.

## Authorship and intention

I stopped formal work to take care of my parents. But I am still writing. I am not stopping.

I realised there are so many things that need to be said out loud — not just to normalise it, but to release the shame that's attached like ghosts to the very phrase. I had to do it for myself. But I also found it created shifts for the people around me, especially in caregiving.

It was too draining to manage both — the business and the care I want to give my family right now. So I stopped one. I am going to document the other.

In the meantime, I'll talk about what I saw. No names. Just what I noticed.

It's also because I kept taking calls, and I saw patterns. The patterns I saw — I recognised I have a window to make a difference for my family, or at least try.

The business wasn't my business. But I've been running small businesses, and I do know where the fault lines run across all of them.

This wasn't just a job for me — it was something I enjoyed, because the foreign domestic workers could be me. Getting to know them, over time, made me realise they are also like me — just born in a different country, with different circumstances. But here we are. It is what it is. My family hired many Filipino helpers, so we could do other things with our time.
