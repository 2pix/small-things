This is part of The Black Cat — a novel in pieces.

The space felt gentle on my body.

Open. Familiar. The kind of easy you don't question because nothing in it asks you to. I could move through it without checking first. Change my mind halfway through a sentence and find the sentence had already adjusted. Follow an impulse past where I'd usually stop and discover there was still ground under me, still warmth, still the particular quality of air that means someone has made room and intends to keep making it.

My shoulders stayed loose.

I didn't notice that either.

My breath came and went the way breath comes and goes when nothing is asking it to be different — slow, unremarkable, the body doing what it does when it's been given permission to just be a body. I moved through the days the way you move through a house you know in the dark. Without looking. Without needing to.

Nothing asked me to stop.

I took that as safety.

The warmth was real. I want to be accurate about that because the rest of this is about what I got wrong and it would be easy to go back and make the warmth a lie too. It wasn't. I felt it as something settling in my chest, a loosening I hadn't known was held until it released. As pull. As the particular sense of rightness that arrives when something fits — not because you made it fit, not because you're performing fit, but because the thing and the space genuinely correspond.

Loving felt honest.

I trusted it would take care of the rest.

I didn't look down.

What I didn't feel was the holding.

You don't, when you're not the one doing it. The held space feels like freedom. Like the ground meeting you each time because ground does that, because that's just what ground is, solid and reliable and asking nothing in return for its reliability. You don't feel it in your legs. You don't feel it in the ease of your own moving. The ease is just — there. Available. The way good light is available. You don't think about where it comes from.

Someone else is making the light.

Someone else is doing the holding.

Plans softened around me. Timing shifted, adjusted, made room. There was always space for one more need, one more impulse I didn't think to question because nothing pushed back, because nothing said wait, I need a moment, wait, I'm tired, wait, I'm not sure I have room for this today. The quiet was so consistent I stopped hearing it as a choice. Started hearing it as air. As the natural atmosphere of this particular space.

I kept going.

I was so comfortable I didn't notice I was moving.

Care is different from love. I know this now in my body the way I know things that arrived too late — as a kind of permanent ache in the place the knowledge should have been.

Love warms. It genuinely warms. I don't take that back.

But warmth isn't attention. Warmth can blaze while attention sleeps. I loved and the loving felt like enough, felt like the whole thing, felt like if I just kept feeling it correctly the rest would follow. I didn't understand that love without care is a fire in a room with no one checking the walls.

Care listens past the warmth. It notices the particular quality of someone's quiet — not rest, not ease, but management. The way a person holds themselves when they're absorbing something they haven't named yet. The slight forward lean of a body that's been bracing. The way someone laughs just a half-beat after they should, the pause where the energy should have been immediate but had to be summoned instead. The stillness that looks like calm but costs.

You mistake calm for capacity.

I did. I watched someone hold everything steady and read the steadiness as room. As spaciousness. As the natural texture of a person who had plenty and was giving from plenty.

I didn't see the holding was the thing they were doing instead of everything else.

The gap didn't announce itself.

That's the part I return to. The part that sits in me still with the particular weight of things that are true and late.

It widened slowly.

A pause I didn't notice skip. A step taken without checking. One more thing continued past where it should have stopped, past where I would have stopped if I'd been watching, if I'd been caring instead of just loving, if I'd asked can you hold this before I handed it over.

Nothing dramatic.

Just a small distance opening between my movement and their ground.

Then one step didn't land.

No sound. No collapse. Just wrongness. The particular wrongness of reaching for something that has always been there and finding the place where it was.

That's when I felt it.

The drop.

Soft. Certain. A hollow opening in my chest where the warmth had been, my legs suddenly aware of themselves in a way they hadn't been when the ground was reliable. The sense of having been standing on something that required someone else's effort to exist.

I hadn't meant to take.

Meaning doesn't change what happened.

The space had never been neutral. The ease had never been free. My ability to keep going, to follow impulse, to move without checking — all of it had rested on effort I wasn't carrying, wasn't seeing, wasn't asking about. Someone had been holding the ground up to meet my feet.

And had stopped being able to.

That was when the dungeon showed itself.

Not around me.

Under me.

In the way the ground stopped adjusting to my weight.

You don't realise love isn't enough until someone can't keep absorbing you.

Until things continue when someone needed them to stop.

Until you never thought to ask.

Love was real.

Care was late.

The door was always open.

I just never noticed someone was holding it.
