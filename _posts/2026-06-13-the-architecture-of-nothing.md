My children and I no longer speak.

Two husbands.

I will leave it at that.

{% include divider.html %}

David arrived the way most things arrive when you're not looking. He needed somewhere to be one afternoon. Then another. Sometimes two weeks. Then it was just the shape of it.

Mark came later. Quieter. He needed somewhere to put his body.

I didn't treat them like my children. I didn't need to replace what I had lost. I was just on the ground with them. The same ground. Doing what was in front of us.

We got along.

{% include divider.html %}

I read stars for a living.

Not stars — the arrangement of things at the moment of a birth. The pattern they make from here. From this angle. From a life.

What people don't understand about a constellation is that it isn't real.

The stars in it are not close to each other. They are not the same age. Some of them are already gone — what you're seeing is light that left before your grandmother was born. They are at different depths in the sky, pointing nowhere near each other in actual space.

What they share is this: seen from here, they make a shape.

That's all a constellation is. A shape that makes sense from one particular place.

{% include divider.html %}

People ask how I built mine.

They mean: when did you decide? What was the plan? How do you create people who stay?

The premise underneath the question is that there is a design phase, and then an implementation phase. That the architect draws first and then builds.

{% include divider.html %}

My children are in the sky. Somewhere. Moving on their own trajectories, which I cannot read from here.

David and Mark are in the kitchen.

I didn't summon them. I didn't place them. I stayed on my own ground, in my own flat, doing what I was doing. And they moved into range.

Not by design. By proximity and mass. By what is already there, pulling quietly.

You do not create the gravity. You either have it or you don't. You either stay on the ground or you don't.

I stayed.

{% include divider.html %}

The lie is in the word built.

As if I drew a plan. As if I knew what I was making.

I made tea. I read charts. I let people arrive the way they actually were.

I am saying the structure you are asking how to build is probably already running. In what you keep showing up for. In the people who have found their way to your table without being asked.

The question is not: how do I build this?

The question is: what are you the gravity of?

And yes, they will arrive.

A constellation is what you name it after. Not before.
